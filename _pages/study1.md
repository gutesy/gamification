By Sean Gates
-

Citation
-
Morschheuser, Benedikt, et al. “Increasing intranet usage through gamification -- insights from an experiment in the banking industry.” 2015 48th Hawaii International Conference on System Sciences, Jan. 2015, pp. 635–642, https://doi.org/10.1109/hicss.2015.83.

THE STUDY
-
This paper looks at how financial institutions used gamification techniques to train its employees to more effectively use its ‘intranet’, which is described as “information systems that help to acquire, memory, distribute and interpret information and knowledge in organizations” (Morchheuser et al., 2015). Essentially, an intranet is a private internet for institutions that want to share sensitive information privately amongst themselves. Since the institutions using this intranet were banks, it can be reasonably understood that it is important for employees to be confident using this software in order to most effectively and securely manage what many people hold near and dear to their hearts– their wallets.

The study conducted an experiment to see if employees using a ‘gamified’ intranet became more motivated to use the intranet compared to employees who did not use a gamified intranet. The experimental version of the intranet version gave individual users quizzes to take after reading articles on the intranet. Participants in the experimental group were rewarded with points for both opening articles and answering quiz questions correctly. For the experimental group, points were displayed on a leaderboard for other participants of the experimental group to see. 

All members of the control and experimental groups were given 10 minutes to read through articles on the intranet in preparation for an imaginary client. The timer was visible to the participants, and whenever they liked, they could log off the intranet before the timer ran out. Once the timer expired, they were then asked questions about themselves (age, sex, number of assets they manage, etc.) for the researchers to collect additional data to assess. The researchers hypotheses were that employees in the experimental group would spend more time on the intranet, spend more time reading each article, read more total articles, answer more quiz questions in total, and answer more quiz questions correctly. In other words, the researchers predicted that the gamified intranet would make employees engage more with the intranet.

The results of the study show that gamification elements increase how much time employees spend engaging with the intranet, and also how actively they get involved, as those with points and a leaderboard ended up performing significantly better on quizzes, on average, compared to employees in the control group. The biggest limitation to this study was the sample size, which at 68 participants, is very small. The researchers suggest that gamification could be effective in other professional fields, such as travel agencies or insurance companies.

MY PROBLEMS
-
The inclusion of a leaderboard to compare yourself against co-workers seems like it could momentarily inspire people to work harder, but over time increase insecurity and a toxic work environment where people are constantly comparing themselves against others to boost their own numbers. I could imagine this leading to burnout and less satisfaction with one’s own career. However, this doesn’t necessarily have any scientific backing– just my two cents. The study itself was conducted fine.
