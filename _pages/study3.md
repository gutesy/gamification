By Chenxi Jiang
-

Citation
Bonde, M. T., Makransky, G., Wandall, J., Larsen, M. V., Morsing, M., Jarmer, H., & Sommer, M. O. A. (2014). Improving biotech education through gamified laboratory simulations. Nature Biotechnology, 32*(7), 694–697.  
https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.2955
-



Summary
-
This study investigated whether gamified laboratory simulations could improve both learning outcomes and student motivation in biotechnology education. The researchers developed a 3D, game-based simulation platform called Labster, which includes storytelling, virtual characters, scoring systems, and realistic lab procedures. They tested two scenarios: a crime-scene lab using PCR and gel electrophoresis, and a genetic engineering lab involving cloning and fermentation. The results showed that gamified simulations led to significantly higher learning gains compared to traditional lectures and also increased students' interest in pursuing biotech careers. The authors conclude that such simulations, especially when combined with conventional teaching, offer a powerful tool for modern science education.

Methods
-
The study used a crossover design with 91 college students in an introductory life science course. All participants completed a 40-question multiple-choice pre-test. Students were divided into two groups: Group A received a traditional lecture first, followed by the gamified simulation; Group B completed the simulation first, then the lecture. Mid-tests and post-tests were administered after each session, and a retention test was given 40 days later. Test data were analyzed using a two-parameter logistic item response theory model. In addition, motivation and interest were measured through surveys of 149 students from multiple high schools and universities.

Results
-
The simulation alone produced a 1.48 standard deviation gain in test scores, compared to 0.84 for traditional lectures — a 76% higher learning improvement (p < 0.0005). The combination of simulation and lecture yielded the highest overall scores. Learning was retained after 40 days, with no significant drop in performance. In motivation surveys, 97% of students found the simulation interesting, 86% preferred it over ordinary exercises, and 44% became more likely to consider a biotech career after using the simulation.

Why We Chose It
-
Our group chose this study because it addresses a real and urgent problem in science education: low student engagement and limited access to expensive lab equipment. Unlike many theoretical papers, this study provides strong quantitative evidence that gamification and simulation can work in practice. We were also impressed by the rigorous crossover design and long-term retention testing. As students interested in biotechnology and education, we find the idea of combining storytelling, 3D animations, and hands-on virtual labs highly inspiring. This study shows that learning can be both effective and enjoyable — a principle we want to promote through our website.
